And Then, the Headphones Come Off

Creative non-fiction essay · Creative Writing class

The drive home was quieter. Not in a bad way; the kind where everyone was tired from having so much fun. Fifi was already dozing off in the back, and Haikal was absentmindedly scrolling through TikTok, his face illuminated by the bright screen of his phone. I didn’t have data on my phone, and the roads of USJ were familiar enough that they were nothing new to gawk at, so I directed my attention to the small screen attached to the dashboard.

To be honest, I don’t know what Hasya’s actual taste in music is. She switched universities (how ironic) three weeks into the first semester, and though we’ve met up with her a couple of times since then, the conversation was always about what she’d been up to recently — what we’d been up to recently — and if things were going well in her classes. There wasn’t a right time to ask about something as insignificant as her music taste.

But I do know what she played that night. As I watched the raindrops race across the passenger window, the heavy, atmospheric guitar riffs of Deftones’ “Be Quiet and Drive (Far Away)” filled the quiet cabin. It wasn’t the polished, choreographed pop I had grown used to. It was gritty, layered, and entirely new to me. Hasya probably doesn’t realise it — she probably just had a random Spotify playlist on shuffle — but she was the one who handed me the key out of my echo chamber.

Dragon Age: Origins

Game review · Backloggd

Wow, finally, a proper review/ramble session! Hopefully the first of many more.

Going in, I admit I had pretty high expectations for the game. Beyond the universal praise, my own best friend AND brother are also massive shooters for the franchise as a whole and have been trying to get me into the games LOL.

Read the rest

Though I'll preface this now by saying I used console commands (pc_immortal and killallhostiles my beloved) to make the combat less of a pain to get through and I honestly cannot recommend it enough. Once I didn't have to think about the combat, my enjoyment of the game increased by a million percent.

Personally, I place a lot of importance on the characters, maybe even more than the plot. If I don't enjoy these characters I'll be traveling/interacting with for the next 50 hours, I'll still play, probably, but I'll be complaining for most of it. Thankfully, I adored the cast (and I totally understand why people still sing praises for this specific game's cast). Alistair's a fan favourite for a reason, and I unexpectedly found myself absolutely loving Leliana. I romanced Zevran and found it sweet but a bit lacking (his personal "quest" is just a random encounter in Denerim? No build-up, no journal entry, nothing. Really?), which was unfortunate. Shale and Wynne were some of my favourites from the unromancables, but I generally liked them all. Despite the companion quests being short, I think the game as a whole - through the overarching plot, party banter, camp conversations - did an amazing job of characterising that I already had a pretty good grasp on their quirks and personalities even before doing the quests.

Playing as a Female Elf Circle of Magi Origin was an unexpectedly fitting pick for my first playthrough. I loved how reactive everyone was to my character being what I lovingly call "subaltern" (the most marginalised group). As the game progressed, I was a little envious of how involved other Origins seemed to be. All the family names seemed to hold a lot of significance but my own (Surana) never came up. But from a meta viewpoint, it's perfect. My character doesn't have an established place in the world - being locked up in the tower for all her life and all - so she and I are kind of experiencing everything for the first time together.

It's also through this origin that I discovered my new reason to power through the games: Cullen :P I love a yearner. I love a dorky little blond guy who goes through something traumatising and comes back two games later as a jaded, hardened man. (Yeah, my favourite character is Leon Kennedy. How'd you know?) I do think it's a missed opportunity to not have any other interactions with him after the events of the Broken Circle quest though. I mean bro indirectly confessed + my character made the decision he was actively fighting against. But alas. I'm greedy and I just wanted more Cullen lol.

Okay, tangent over. I loved the plot - I'm a sucker for the classic hero's journey narrative - and the individual faction quests were a lot of fun (though I personally would take the Fade over the Deep Roads any day). The Dalish quest was the weakest to me personally, but I also get that elves are a whole aren't really that much of a focal point in the first game. I will say that the moral/big choices you have to make in each quest were a nice touch and enhanced the roleplaying aspect of it. Like my character choosing Bhelen as king but killing Branka because she pragmatically believes a cruel tyrant will do the dwarves a favour, but draws the line at pouring lyrium in people's eyes and turning them into golems LOL. The side quests did a great job of breathing life into the world and its inhabitants too, and combining that with the reactivity of the NPCs made for a really immersive experience.

The game isn't perfect by any means, and there's definitely some questionable aspects that could've been handled better (cough the Ritual cough) but it didn't make me enjoy the game any less. I was satisfied with the endings I got (as close to what I'd personally consider as a "good" ending I guess) and I loved seeing them all laid out like that at the end. All in all, I had a blast playing and I can't wait to finish up Awakening and Witch Hunt :D I know 2 isn't as universally beloved but I'm excited for it nonetheless!

Dragon Age II

Game review · Backloggd

Another day, another Dragon Age game completed. This one only took about 2 weeks to finish, which is 39 hours of playtime with all DLCs as well. In contrast to DAO, I went into this knowing absolutely nothing about this game (since literally no one talks about it) and I fell in love almost immediately :P

Read the rest

It has its faults, of course - companions are barely given time to shine individually, Anders' feels like he's moving at a different pace than everyone, the Qunari plot in Act 2 didn't get the proper build-up it deserved, so on and so forth - but in general, a lot of things people (understandably) didn't like were things I found myself appreciating.

Aspects like not having to change your companions' armours and the dialogue wheel come to mind. I also really enjoyed the pre-made backstory despite being a "make my OC and try to imitate her canon lore as much as possible" enjoyer. Hawke isn't quite mine the way the Warden is, but I felt just as attached to her and her journey, and I appreciated the acts as a means to show time progressing (even if nothing seemed that way visually lol).

Maybe I'm easy to please but I find the story wasn't too bad. DA2 made me love the whole "Mages vs. Templars" discourse. With all the crazy stuff the mages are up to, maybe templars are needed to keep them in check... but would those extremes even exist if mages' entire lives were still to be dictated and controlled by another? But people will always be drawn to power regardless, won't they? Gah. It's too good. It's my postcolonial studies in a new flavour and it's delicious.

But we can't talk about templars and not talk about THE templar. So it's Cullen Time. We're at the "recurring NPC" stage of his existence where he gets a whole quest and you get a glimpse at a part of him that hasn't been swept away by his newfound mage-hating pastime - his dorkiness<3 He gets flustered about talking to the brothel ladies and makes Hawke do it instead. It's glorious and it was all the fuel I needed to keep going. And I really did appreciate seeing his little developments over the years; it was only natural he'd have such deep disdain for mages with everything he's been through, but his own morals eventually allow him to see Meredith's faults and have a more nuanced opinion on the matter. A nice little transition that Inquisition picks up beautifully (hehe).

Anyway. I will say that the combat is the absolute best in this, hands down. In Origins I became well-acquainted with the "runscript killallhostiles" command, whereas here I actually WANTED to invest in a good build and strategize because everything was so fluid. Companion-wise I think Origins still can't be beat, but it's less of the companions' fault and more on how they went about companion interactions this time around. I absolutely adored Varric and Hawke's dynamic. Isabela's fun (and I love the "I can't believe you made me do a good thing, ew" trope), Merill's a weird cutie, Fenris is just....<3 And Anders was there.

All this to say that I honestly enjoyed DA2 despite everything. Really, my only issues with the game are the pacing and the lack of companion moments. But I loved the core conflict and I loved Hawke and the combat was so good it almost makes up for everything else :P And if I'm being honest, knowing Cullen's waiting for me in Inquisition made the rush better lol. To work!
